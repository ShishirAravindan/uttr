# Design Notes

Extended arguments about what uttr is and why it is shaped this way. These are essays, not specifications — they exist to make the reasoning behind the product legible, including the reasoning nobody performed deliberately at the time.

They are written to be read in order, but each stands alone.

**[`axes-and-modes.md`](axes-and-modes.md)** — Why speech-to-text products appear to come in two kinds (push-to-talk and dictate), why that appearance is a coincidence mistaken for a category, and what it costs to have inherited an interaction model rather than chosen one. Introduces the three axes — delimitation, feedback, delivery — and locates uttr on them.

**[`speech-and-typing.md`](speech-and-typing.md)** — Against the claim that voice replaces the keyboard. Takes the strongest evidence for it seriously, shows why the inference fails, and proposes *formedness* — how complete a thought is before you start producing it — as the variable that actually predicts which input serves you.

**[`ambient.md`](ambient.md)** — What "ambient" should mean if the word is to do any work, proposed as a test rather than a mood: a technology is ambient to the degree that it costs nothing when you are not using it. Examines three of uttr's decisions against that test, and is honest about where the application does not yet pass.

The operational consequences — the actual backlog — live in [`../operational-notes.md`](../operational-notes.md).
