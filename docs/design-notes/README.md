# Design Notes

Extended arguments about what uttr is and why it is shaped this way. These are essays rather than specifications. They exist to make the reasoning behind the product legible, including the reasoning nobody performed deliberately at the time.

Each stands alone, though they argue with each other in places, and those disagreements are left in rather than smoothed over.

**[`axes-and-modes.md`](axes-and-modes.md)** — Why speech-to-text products appear to come in two kinds, why that appearance is a coincidence mistaken for a category, and what it costs to have inherited an interaction model rather than chosen one. Introduces delimitation, feedback and delivery as axes, locates uttr on them, and closes on two ways the map fails: it has no coordinate for the transformation step that most distinguishes uttr's competitors, and the axes turn out to be coupled.

**[`speech-and-typing.md`](speech-and-typing.md)** — Against the claim that voice replaces the keyboard. Takes the strongest evidence for it seriously, separates the study from the inference drawn from it, and proposes *formedness* — how complete a thought is before you produce it — as the better variable. Confronts the objection that a language model between recognizer and insertion dissolves much of the argument, and concedes most of it.

**[`ambient.md`](ambient.md)** — What "ambient" should mean if the word is to do work, proposed as a test rather than a mood: a technology is ambient to the degree that it costs nothing outside the moments you intend to use it. Examines the keystroke-monitoring refusal, the menu-bar-only surface, and on-device inference against that test, is explicit about the criterion's own weak points, and names two places the application currently violates it.

The operational consequences live in [`../operational-notes.md`](../operational-notes.md).
